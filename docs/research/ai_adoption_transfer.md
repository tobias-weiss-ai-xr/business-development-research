# AI Adoption Corpus — Lessons Transferable to Other Projects

**Corpus:** `ai-adoption` category, 380 papers (2021–2026, growth 12→46→63→85→111
papers/year — the fastest-accelerating category besides B2B marketing)
**Author:** Tobias Weiss
**Purpose:** Answer one question: *what can we learn from the ai-adoption corpus
and transfer to our other projects?*

---

## TL;DR

1. **Training is the mediating variable between AI adoption and outcomes** —
   the corpus's strongest finding. This is the empirical backbone of the ALaaS
   value proposition: it belongs on the landing page, in sales conversations
   and in the newsletter.
2. **AI literacy is a staged continuum, not a binary** — evidence says people
   cluster at *avoidance* or *uncritical reliance*; ALaaS tiers should be
   designed as a progression through stages, with task-oriented (not generic)
   assessment.
3. **The biggest research gaps are the best business opportunities** —
   `ai-adoption/development` (5 papers) and `ai-adoption/evaluation` (5 papers)
   are almost empty. Nobody has yet written the definitive guides on *how to
   implement* and *how to measure* AI literacy programs. That is white space
   for KI-Kompetenz-Training content and offers.
4. **The pipeline pattern transfers** — the taxonomy → momentum → burst → gap
   method used here can be reapplied to any new vertical (e.g. an EU-AI-Act
   corpus, an AI-tools corpus) and to the other research repos.

## 1. What the Corpus Says (Evidence Synthesis)

### 1.1 Adoption without training underdelivers

The single most transferable paper is *Artificial intelligence adoption and
workplace training* (Muehlemann, Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization,
2025-09, [doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2025.107206](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2025.107206)):
German establishment-level panel data 2019–2023 showing how AI adoption
reshapes training strategies and which firms actually build a skilled
workforce. The causal chain in the literature: **AI investment → complementary
skills investment → performance**, not AI investment → performance directly.

The same mediation result appears at European SME scale: *Artificial
intelligence adoption and revenue growth in European SMEs: synergies with IoT
and big data analytics* (Internet Research, 2024-12,
[doi.org/10.1108/intr-02-2024-0195](https://doi.org/10.1108/intr-02-2024-0195))
explicitly confronts "the conventional notion that adopting AI positively
affects firm performance" with failure cases — growth materializes only when
AI is combined with complementary capabilities.

**Transfer:** the ALaaS pitch is not "AI is coming, get ready" but "AI
adoption only pays off with complementary training — we supply the
complementary training, evidenced by large-scale panel data."

### 1.2 SME barriers are organizational, not technical

Survey research using TOE–DOI frameworks (Technology–Organization–
Environment, Diffusion of Innovation) consistently ranks missing skills top:
*Artificial Intelligence Adoption in SMEs: Survey Based on TOE–DOI Framework*
(Applied Sciences, 2025-06,
[doi.org/10.3390/app15126465](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15126465)) plus
multiple SME surveys in the corpus (Jordanian MSMEs, GCC workforce 2025-11,
barriers-and-benefits studies).

**Transfer:** sales content and the audit tool should measure *organizational*
readiness (skills, processes, management support, data) — not just tool
knowledge. The corpus says the failure mode is organizational.

### 1.3 AI literacy: staged, task-oriented, occupation-aligned

Three converging papers:

- *Beyond Tool Adoption: A Practical Five-Stage Developmental Continuum for AI
  Literacy in Higher Education* (2026-04,
  [arxiv.org/abs/2606.00038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00038)) — students
  cluster at two extremes: *avoidance* (fear, mistrust, ethical concern) and
  *uncritical reliance*. Literacy programs must move people through stages,
  and the extremes define the curriculum.
- *AI Literacy Assessment Revisited: A Task-Oriented Approach Aligned with
  Real-world Occupations* (2025-11,
  [arxiv.org/abs/2511.05475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05475)) — prevailing
  literacy definitions misalign with actual work tasks; assessment must be
  per-occupation.
- *Gender Differences in AI Literacy Workshop Outcomes and Deepfake Engagement*
  (2026-05, [arxiv.org/abs/2606.14718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14718)) —
  workshop outcomes differ systematically by cohort; one-size-fits-all formats
  leave subgroups behind.

**Transfer:** ALaaS tiers should be a staged progression (e.g. Aware →
Apply → Evaluate → Create), delivered **per role/occupation**, with
task-oriented assessment — and outcome measurement must be cohort-aware.

### 1.4 The "training trap" and expectation recalibration

- *Training for Obsolescence? The AI-Driven Education Trap* (2025-08,
  [arxiv.org/abs/2508.19625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19625)) warns that training
  can prepare people for jobs that AI is dissolving. Curriculum must track
  *evolving task composition*, not current tool features.
- *Persona Migration and Expectation Recalibration in Generative AI Adoption*
  (2026-07, [arxiv.org/abs/2607.13798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13798)) —
  longitudinal public-sector study: users migrate through personas and must
  recalibrate expectations. Managing expectations is part of adoption success
  (and churn prevention).

**Transfer:** ALaaS onboarding should explicitly recalibrate expectations
(what AI can/cannot do per use case) and the curriculum should teach
adaptability/meta-skills, not only today's tools. The "cognitive commons" risk
paper (*The Tragedy of the Cognitive Commons*, 2026-07,
[arxiv.org/abs/2607.29380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29380)) supplies the
"why human expertise still matters" argument for newsletters and
thought-leadership pieces.

### 1.5 Readiness is per-task, and strategy is task-selection

- *Task-Level AI Readiness Assessment for Business Process Management: The
  T-IPO Model and LARA Matrix* (2026-04,
  [arxiv.org/abs/2605.16297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16297)) — readiness
  varies per task; assess at task granularity.
- *AI Strategy: How to Choose What AI Product to Implement* (2026-07,
  [arxiv.org/abs/2607.23733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23733)) — the core
  strategic act is choosing *what to automate/augment*.
- *Dynamic Capabilities for AI-Enabled Exploration* (2026-07,
  [arxiv.org/abs/2607.02645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02645)) — capability
  building precedes innovation outcomes.

**Transfer:** the free audit offering should produce a **per-task readiness
report** — that is both a better lead magnet than generic checklists and a
direct sales opener ("here are the 3 tasks where training pays off first").

### 1.6 Regulation is now a driver (and a complexity risk)

EU-AI-Act-related terms burst at 3.2× in the 12-month window (3 of 4 corpus
papers with "eu ai act" are from the last 12 months; `regulat*` appears in 23
recent ai-adoption papers). But *"Death by a thousand taxonomies?": AI Risk
Classification in Practice* (2026-08,
[arxiv.org/abs/2608.06831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06831)) documents how
complex risk classification is in practice.

**Transfer:** the EU-AI-Act story strengthens the "literacy as compliance"
argument (Art. 4 literacy obligations) — and the taxonomy-complexity paper
justifies ALaaS as the *practical interpreter*: we turn regulatory complexity
into per-role training paths. This is a defensible, differentiated position.

## 3. Cross-Project Structural Lessons

1. **Evidence → offer loop:** corpus gaps predict offer demand. The empty
   cells (`ai-adoption/development`, `ai-adoption/evaluation`) are where
   competitors have no evidence to cite — first-mover content wins.
2. **Bursts predict conversation openings:** agentic, solo, eu-ai-act,
   ai-literacy, genai are the 12-month burst keywords — they open sales
   conversations ("your competitors are asking about X") as well as articles.
3. **Survey vs panel evidence hierarchy:** marketing material should cite
   panel/econometric studies (Muehlemann; Internet Research) over surveys —
   the corpus itself shows surveys dominate SME research, so robust panel
   evidence is a differentiator.
4. **One pipeline, many verticals:** the 20×8 taxonomy + momentum + bursts +
   gaps + README generation stack is now proven in three repos
   (learning-research, graph-research, business-development-research).
